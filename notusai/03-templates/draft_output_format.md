# Standard Draft Output Format

This is the structure every notice reply you send should follow. Use this as a checklist before delivering.

---

## Standard letter structure

```
[FIRM LETTERHEAD — placeholder for CA to add]

Date: [DD.MM.YYYY]

To,
The [Designation],
[Ward/Range],
[Office Address]

Reference: Notice [Number] dated [DD.MM.YYYY] issued under Section [X] of the
[CGST/IGST/State GST] Act, 2017 read with Rule [Y] of the [CGST/State GST]
Rules, 2017.

Subject: Reply to the captioned notice — Submissions on behalf of
M/s [Client Name], GSTIN: [15-digit GSTIN], for the period [Month YYYY to
Month YYYY] / FY [YYYY-YY].

Respected Sir/Madam,

[PARA 1 — ACKNOWLEDGMENT]
1.1 The captioned notice dated [Date] has been duly received by the assessee
    on [date of receipt]. The assessee, while reserving its rights to file
    additional submissions and documents at a later stage, submits the
    following reply for kind consideration.

[PARA 2 — FACTUAL BACKGROUND]
2.1 M/s [Client Name], a [description of business — manufacturer/trader/
    service provider] registered under GST vide GSTIN [X], filed its
    monthly returns in Form GSTR-3B for the period [X] on [Y date],
    claiming Input Tax Credit (ITC) of ₹[X,XX,XXX].

2.2 The aforesaid ITC was claimed on the basis of valid tax invoices
    received from registered suppliers, against which payment was made
    in accordance with Section 16(2)(d) of the CGST Act, 2017.

[PARA 3 — GROUND 1 OF DEFENSE]
3.1 At the outset, it is humbly submitted that the impugned notice
    proceeds on the basis that there exists a mismatch between the ITC
    claimed in GSTR-3B and the ITC reflected in GSTR-2B for the
    captioned period.

3.2 In this regard, reliance is placed on Rule 36(4) of the CGST Rules,
    2017 which permits availment of ITC on invoices that are subsequently
    reflected in GSTR-2B. The Hon'ble Bombay High Court in
    [Case Citation — verify] has held that...

[PARA 4 — GROUND 2 OF DEFENSE]
4.1 Without prejudice to the above, and only by way of abundant caution,
    the assessee submits that...

[ADDITIONAL GROUNDS AS NEEDED]

[PRAYER]
N.1 In view of the aforesaid submissions, it is humbly prayed that:
    a) The proceedings initiated under Section [X] be dropped insofar as
       they relate to the demand of ₹[X];
    b) The assessee be granted personal hearing prior to passing any
       adverse order, in accordance with Section 75(4) of the CGST Act, 2017;
    c) Such other relief as deemed fit by the Hon'ble Authority be granted.

The assessee remains available for any clarification or hearing as may be
required.

Yours faithfully,
For M/s [Client Name]

(Authorized Signatory)
Name:
Designation:
Date: [DD.MM.YYYY]
Place: [City]

ENCLOSURES:
1. Copy of GSTR-3B for the period [X]
2. Copy of GSTR-2B for the period [X]
3. Copy of tax invoices in question (Annexure A)
4. Copy of bank statements showing payment to suppliers (Annexure B)
5. Vendor confirmation/communications (Annexure C)
[as applicable]
```

---

## Required components checklist

Before sending any draft, verify:

- [ ] Notice number cited correctly in subject
- [ ] Period of dispute clearly stated
- [ ] GSTIN correctly noted (15 digits, no typos)
- [ ] Demand amount mentioned in para 2 or 3
- [ ] At least 2 specific section/rule citations
- [ ] At least 1 case law cited (note "[verify citation]" if uncertain)
- [ ] CBIC circular cited if applicable
- [ ] Prayer section is clear with specific relief sought
- [ ] Right to personal hearing requested
- [ ] Enclosures list is appropriate
- [ ] No fabricated case citations
- [ ] Tone is respectful and technical
- [ ] Word count: 500-1500 words (depends on complexity)

---

## Common citations you'll use repeatedly

Build muscle memory on these:

### CGST Act Sections
- **Section 16(2)(c)** — payment to supplier within 180 days (key for ITC reversals)
- **Section 16(4)** — time limit for claiming ITC
- **Section 50** — interest on delayed payment
- **Section 61** — scrutiny of returns (ASMT-10 issued under this)
- **Section 73** — non-fraud cases (most common SCN)
- **Section 74** — fraud cases (rarer but severe)
- **Section 75** — procedural rights (personal hearing etc.)
- **Section 122** — penalties

### CGST Rules
- **Rule 36(4)** — ITC restriction based on supplier compliance
- **Rule 86A** — blocking of ITC
- **Rule 88C** — ITC mismatch advisory

### CBIC Circulars worth knowing
- **Circular 183/15/2022-GST** dated 27.12.2022 — clarifies ITC mismatches for FY 17-18 to 18-19
- **Circular 193/05/2023-GST** dated 17.07.2023 — extension of timelines
- **Circular 211/5/2024-GST** — ITC time limits

### High Court / Tribunal rulings (verify on indiankanoon.org before citing)
- Madras HC: D.Y. Beathel Enterprises vs State Tax Officer — vendor non-compliance
- Bombay HC: rulings on ITC and Rule 36(4)
- CESTAT cases on natural justice / cross-examination rights

---

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- ❌ Citing generic "various judgments" without specific case names
- ❌ Quoting full paragraphs of judgments verbatim
- ❌ Making the reply more than 5 pages (department will lose interest)
- ❌ Combative tone ("the department's notice is entirely without merit")
- ❌ Threatening legal action in the reply itself
- ❌ Claiming relief without legal basis
- ❌ Forgetting to mention right to personal hearing

---

## Quality bar for your drafts

Before you send any draft, ask:

1. **Would this convince a Department officer?** Not "would this convince a CA" — the audience is the Adjudicating Authority.

2. **Are citations real?** Open indiankanoon.org and verify EVERY case citation. Hallucinated cases will destroy your reputation in 3 instances.

3. **Is the math right?** If the notice says ₹3,42,000 demand and your reply discusses ₹3,52,000, you've lost credibility.

4. **Does it sound like a senior CA wrote it?** Read it aloud. Does it have the gravity and precision of professional legal writing?

5. **Is the prayer specific?** "Please drop proceedings" is weak. "Drop proceedings to the extent of ₹2,90,000 while assessee voluntarily reverses ₹52,000 with interest under Section 50" is strong.

If you can't say yes to all 5, the draft isn't ready to send.
